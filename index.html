          A model-based reflex agent approaching a junction

          The blue car is an agent. Every 0.2 s it perceives the signal, the gap to any obstacle, its own speed and the road surface. It decides by checking condition–action rules in priority order, then acts by braking, holding speed or accelerating. Its internal model remembers how long the light has been amber.

          Facts, rules and forward chaining

          Conditions become propositional facts. IF–THEN rules derive new facts until nothing more can be inferred (forward chaining). The derived facts select the recommendations. Toggle facts yourself, or load them from the live simulation.

                An agent that learns a speed policy from feedback

                For each road condition the agent picks a cruising speed, drives a simulated trip and receives a reward: progress for arriving sooner, minus a noisy hazard penalty that grows with the risk score and with the square of the speed. It updates its value estimates Q(condition, speed) and gradually prefers speeds that pay off (ε-greedy learning).

                  A* search for the safest route across the city grid

                  Grey cells are roads, beige cells are buildings. Entering a road cell costs 1 + hazard penalty (waterlogging when rainy, fog banks when visibility is low, congestion when traffic is dense, potholes, blackspots when risk is high). Works zones are blocked. A* expands the node with the lowest f(n) = g(n) + h(n).

                  Click mode

                  Hover or move the cursor over a cell to see g, h and f.

                  Heuristic: h(n) = Manhattan distance to the destination. Every move costs at least 1, so h never overestimates (admissible) and never drops by more than a step cost (consistent). The first time A* expands the destination, its path cost is optimal.

                  How the educational risk model works

                  A hand-written points model, deterministic and fully documented. It is not trained on accident data and it is not the Random Forest from the Python version of this project.

                  Assumptions & limitations

                  • Point values are illustrative judgements, chosen so that speed matters most and hazards compound with speed. They are not fitted to statistics.
                  • The score ranks conditions from safer to riskier. It is not a probability of an accident and has not been validated.
                  • Stopping distance uses a 1.5 s reaction time and typical tyre–road friction (dry 0.7, wet 0.4).
                  • No live traffic, weather or accident data is used anywhere on this page.
                  • The street is an illustrative composite inspired by Coimbatore, not an exact map.

                  Road Risk Simulator

                  A browser version of the AI_Road_Accident_Simulation university micro-project: animated traffic, weather effects, a transparent risk model and seven AI demonstrations, built with HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript.

                  Educational simulation only. Scores and recommendations come from a simplified teaching model, not from real accident data, and must not be used for real-world road-safety decisions. Always follow local traffic law and official guidance.
